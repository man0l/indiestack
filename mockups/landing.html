The mechanism: a Worker that isn’t on your origin.

If the monitor shares the box with the app, it dies with the app — that’s the Kuma trap. IndieStack runs on Cloudflare, not on the VPS it watches. You deploy it to the account you already have. D1, R2, one cron. The Worker is the monitor.

01
ping

Is the site up?

HTTP, TCP, DNS, SSL and domain expiry. Keyword present/absent, expected status, slow-response ceiling. Two failures before a down alert — one blip is not an outage.

02
heartbeat

Did the job run?

Your cron hits /beat/<token>. If it goes quiet past interval + grace, Discord/Slack fires. IndieStack does not run the job. It notices the job did not run.

03
status + logs

Where do I send people?

Public status is / on your Worker. Errors POST to /log/<token> and live 24h in R2 — admin only, never on the status page.

Caps are the product. That’s how you stay on free.

A typical indie — 10 URLs every 5 minutes + a few heartbeats — is a few thousand requests a day. Workers Free is 100k. You will not hit it. These limits exist so you cannot accidentally become a monitoring farm and blow the plan.

20 uptime monitors HTTPS, HTTP, TCP (443, SSH, Postgres, Redis, SMTP), host-up, DNS A/AAAA/MX, SSL expiry, domain expiry. Interval 1–60 min. So 10 URLs × 5 min stays ~6k checks/day. You don’t buy Pro for 60-second checks you won’t use.
